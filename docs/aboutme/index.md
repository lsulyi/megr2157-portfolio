#About Me

My name is Logan Sulyi. I am a third-year Mechanical Engineering student with a Motorsports Concentration at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte. My desire to become an engineer started at a young age, when I would watch NASCAR races every weekend with my parents. I thought it was the coolest thing ever and wanted to be a part of the sport in some way. As for most kids who liked NASCAR at a young age, I dreamed of racing in the NASCAR Cup Series. Fortunately for me, I got to experience an ounce of what drivers do weekly during their career. From 2019-2023, I competed in quarter midgets and full-sized focus midgets along the East Coast. Quarter midgets were more of an introduction to racing, while the full-sized midgets were a big step forward. When dreaming of being a NASCAR driver at a young age, you always think about winning the championship. Although I never raced in NASCAR, I did win the USAC Eastern Midget Championship in 2023, which is a glimpse into what I dreamed about doing as a kid. After my racing career ended, I have now turned to the professional side of the sport, hoping to eventually work in the motorsports industry, which is exactly why I chose to attend Charlotte. As an engineer, I enjoy learning new things, which I find myself doing a lot of every semester at Charlotte. From learning how to code in MATLAB to making my first extrude in SolidWorks, I've found that learning new technical skills is a very tedious but enjoyable process. As challenging as learning these software programs is, using them properly is such a satisfying feeling. I also enjoy hands-on work, as I would work on my race car with my grandfather weekly. Sometimes, it felt like a giant science project, from making adjustments to get the correct cross to cleaning the valves. During my time here at Charlotte so far, I have completed two projects. I completed both during the Spring '26 semester by designing a functional air engine in Creo Parametric and then taking apart and reassembling a 79cc Predator Motor. While there were definitely some challenges, I can take what I learned from making each part and assembling the engine to other CAD software. For the engine project, I got to go up close to see how an engine works, but made simply as a 79cc Predator motor can be found in items like pressure washers or generators. As I continue my education here at Charlotte, I look forward to the new skills I will learn and to becoming a better engineer.

Defending an engineering decision most simply is by explaining why you chose one part for a project over another. Some may say that making these decisions is based purely on preference, but you must weigh your options before deciding. I believe that defending an engineering decision is the ability to analyze a situation and evaluate every aspect, like price, requirements, constraints, and more, to explain why you believe that this part should be used over another part. A lot of time has to go into every decision, and reviewing the pros and cons of both options helps slowly narrow your choices down to only one. I believe I currently lack the skill of defending an engineering decision. Personally, I do look at every option and analyze what does and doesn't work for every possible decision, but then I get stuck. I find it hard to put my foot down and decide what the best possible option is for me. When I get stuck, I find myself ignoring the problem and not doing anything because I become overwhelmed and unsure what the best option is. I think my biggest issue with making these decisions boils down to both a lack of confidence and the fear of making the wrong decision. Making the wrong decision is the bigger problem of the two, as I feel like both options have their strengths and weaknesses, which later causes me to get stuck.
